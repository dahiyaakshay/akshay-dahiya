Off-Page SEO: Building Authority

Backlinks, brand mentions, reviews, and E-E-A-T signals that build the third-party trust search engines and AI systems use to rank and cite your site.

Off-page SEO encompasses all the actions taken outside your own website to improve its position in search results. While on-page SEO controls what happens within your site's code and content, off-page SEO is about how the rest of the internet, and increasingly other platforms and AI systems, perceives, references, and vouches for your site. It's the trust and authority layer of search visibility, and it's often the deciding factor between two pages with similarly strong on-page optimization.

Why Off-Page SEO Matters

Search engines need a way to judge which sites are trustworthy and authoritative on a given topic, independent of what those sites say about themselves. Off-page signals act as third-party validation. A backlink from a respected industry publication, a mention in a well-regarded newsletter, or consistent positive brand sentiment across the web all tell search engines that real people and other credible sources vouch for your content. Ahrefs' analysis of roughly 1 million top-ranking keywords found a Spearman correlation between referring domains and rankings of about 0.22 to 0.24 across all query types. That may sound modest on its own, but given that ranking algorithms weigh hundreds of signals simultaneously, a single factor sitting consistently at that level is considered a meaningful lever.

Backlinks: The Core of Off-Page SEO

Backlinks remain one of the most influential off-page ranking factors. A backlink is a link from another website pointing to yours, and it functions as a vote of confidence. Backlinko's study of 11.8 million search results, still one of the largest publicly available datasets on ranking factors, found that the number one result on Google has 3.8 times more backlinks on average than pages ranking two through 10. Ahrefs' own research puts it plainly: pages with stronger backlink metrics continue to align with higher rankings, even as search algorithms have grown far more sophisticated. Not all backlinks carry equal weight, though. Search engines evaluate:

  • Authority of the linking domain: a link from a well-established, trusted site carries more weight than one from a low-quality or spammy site.
  • Relevance: a link from a site in your industry or topic area is more valuable than an unrelated one.
  • Anchor text: the clickable text used in the link provides context about what the linked page is about, though Ahrefs research has found that exact-match anchor text isn't actually more effective at improving rankings than natural, varied anchor text.
  • Link placement: editorial links embedded naturally within content tend to carry more weight than links buried in footers or sidebars.
  • Follow vs. nofollow: follow links pass authority directly; nofollow links (common in comments, some guest posts, and paid placements) don't pass ranking signal in the same way, though they can still drive referral traffic and brand exposure.

Quality consistently outweighs quantity. Ahrefs' analysis of over 1 billion pages found that 66.31% have zero backlinks at all, and roughly 94% of all published content earns zero external links, meaning only about 6% of content earns even a single backlink. A handful of backlinks from authoritative, relevant sources will do far more for rankings than hundreds of low-quality directory or spun-content links.

Earning Backlinks: Sustainable Strategies

The most durable link-building strategies focus on creating something worth linking to and then making sure the right people know it exists. Effective approaches include:

  • Original research and data: studies, surveys, and proprietary data are highly linkable because journalists and bloggers need sources to cite.
  • Comprehensive guides and tools: content or free tools that solve a real problem attract organic links over time.
  • Digital PR: pitching newsworthy angles, expert commentary, or data stories to journalists and publications. A recent BuzzStream survey found that 85.8% of respondents consider digital PR the most effective tactic for building backlinks today, ahead of brand awareness campaigns and direct traffic generation.
  • Guest contributions: writing for reputable industry publications with a natural, editorially-approved link back to your site.
  • Broken link building: finding broken links on relevant sites and suggesting your content as a replacement.
  • Relationship building: genuine relationships with other site owners, journalists, and industry figures lead to organic mentions and links over time.

Strategies to avoid include buying links in bulk, participating in link farms or private blog networks, and excessive reciprocal linking schemes, all of which violate most search engines' guidelines and risk manual penalties.

Brand Mentions and Unlinked Citations

Search engines have grown increasingly sophisticated at recognizing brand mentions even without a hyperlink. Ahrefs has found a surprising correlation between unlinked branded web mentions and visibility in AI Overviews, one that in some analyses outweighs the correlation seen from referring domains and backlinks alone. Actively monitoring for unlinked brand mentions and reaching out to request a link where appropriate is a low-effort, high-value off-page tactic.

Social Signals and Brand Presence

While likes, shares, and follower counts aren't direct ranking factors in the way backlinks are, social media activity contributes to off-page SEO indirectly. Content that gets shared widely has more opportunity to be seen by people who might link to it, cite it, or reference it elsewhere. A strong, consistent brand presence across social platforms also reinforces the entity signals search engines use to establish who you are, what you're known for, and whether you're a legitimate, active business.

Online Reviews and Local Signals

For businesses with a physical or service-area presence, reviews on platforms like Google Business Profile, Yelp, and industry-specific review sites are a significant off-page factor, particularly for local search visibility. BrightLocal's research consistently shows how much weight consumers place on this signal: 49% of consumers say they trust online reviews as much as personal recommendations from friends and family, and industry survey data from Whitespark suggests review signals make up around 17% of Google Local Pack ranking factors. The volume, recency, rating, and diversity of review sources all contribute to local ranking algorithms, and BrightLocal's own data shows businesses in the Google Map Pack receive substantially more calls and website clicks than those sitting in positions four through 10. Responding to reviews, both positive and negative, also signals active, credible business management; BrightLocal has found that 88% of consumers say they would use a business that replies to all of its reviews.

Influencer and Community Engagement

Getting your brand or content in front of relevant influencers, forum communities, and Q&A platforms can generate both direct referral traffic and secondary backlinks as others discover and reference your content. Genuine participation, answering questions, contributing expertise, and being helpful, tends to outperform overtly promotional activity, both in community reception and in the quality of resulting mentions.

E-E-A-T and Off-Page Signals

Search engines increasingly evaluate Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness (E-E-A-T) as a framework for quality, and off-page signals feed directly into the "Authoritativeness" and "Trustworthiness" components. Author bios linked to credible external profiles, mentions on industry association sites, being cited by recognized experts, and a consistent track record of accurate, high-quality content across the web all reinforce E-E-A-T beyond what any single page's content can demonstrate on its own.

Measuring Off-Page SEO Success

Off-page performance is typically tracked through:

  • Referring domains: the number of unique websites linking to yours, generally more important than total backlink count. Ahrefs has found that pages gain, on average, 5% to 14.5% more referring domains per month as they climb higher in rankings, creating a compounding effect.
  • Domain authority metrics: third-party scores (from tools like Ahrefs, Moz, or Semrush) that estimate a domain's overall link-based authority.
  • Branded search volume: growth in people searching for your brand name directly often correlates with off-page visibility gains.
  • Referral traffic: visits coming from external links, which indicates real audience engagement beyond just SEO value.

Off-Page SEO for Local and Multi-Location Businesses

For businesses tied to a physical location or service area, off-page SEO extends into the local ecosystem of directories, citations, and maps platforms. Consistent name, address, and phone number information across directories builds a trust signal that local search algorithms rely on heavily. BrightLocal's research found that 62% of consumers would avoid using a business if they encountered incorrect information about it online, underscoring how directly citation accuracy can affect conversion.

Competitive Off-Page Analysis

Understanding where competitors earn their authority is a valuable input into off-page strategy. Analyzing a competitor's backlink profile and the publications and platforms where they're active reveals opportunities that might otherwise go unnoticed.

Off-Page SEO in an AI Search Era

As AI-driven search and answer engines become a larger share of discovery, off-page signals are extending into how often and how favorably a brand is referenced across the sources these systems draw from, including review sites, forums, news coverage, and structured data repositories. BrightLocal's 2026 research found that AI tools have jumped from 6% to 45% of local business discovery in a single year, putting them ahead of platforms like Yelp and Tripadvisor and behind only Google and Facebook. Being cited accurately and consistently across the broader web is becoming as important as traditional backlinks for being surfaced in AI-generated answers, not just traditional search results.

Is off-page SEO more important than on-page SEO?
Neither works well without the other. On-page SEO ensures a page can rank and satisfy intent once discovered; off-page SEO builds the authority and trust needed to outrank competitors for competitive terms. Strong content with no authority signals rarely ranks for competitive keywords, and strong authority pointed at thin content underperforms too.
How many backlinks do I need to rank well?
There's no fixed number since it depends on the competitiveness of the target keyword and the strength of competing pages' backlink profiles. Ahrefs data shows the #1 result typically has around 3.8 times more backlinks than lower positions, but a handful of highly relevant, authoritative backlinks can outperform hundreds of low-quality ones.
Are nofollow links worthless for SEO?
No. While nofollow links don't pass ranking authority the same way follow links do, they still contribute to a natural-looking backlink profile, drive referral traffic, and can lead to future follow links or brand exposure. A healthy link profile includes both.
How long does it take for off-page SEO efforts to show results?
Off-page SEO is typically slower than on-page changes. A survey by Authority Hacker found that link builders observe measurable ranking impact from new links in an average of about 3.1 months, with 46.6% seeing effects within one to three months.
Can buying backlinks get my site penalized?
Yes. Purchasing links specifically to manipulate rankings violates most search engines' guidelines and can result in manual penalties or algorithmic devaluation of the purchased links. Sustainable, earned links are lower-risk and more durable long-term.
Do social media shares directly improve rankings?
Social shares aren't a confirmed direct ranking factor, but they increase content visibility, which can lead to more organic backlinks, brand mentions, and search demand, all of which do influence rankings indirectly.
