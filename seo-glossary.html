SEO Glossary

150 essential SEO terms, from technical fundamentals and off-page metrics to the emerging AI search vocabulary every practitioner should know.

ABCDEFGHIKLMNOPQRSTUVWZ

A

301 Redirect
A permanent redirect that sends users and search engines from one URL to another, passing along most of the original page's ranking authority. It's the standard method for handling moved or consolidated content.
302 Redirect
A temporary redirect indicating the move is not permanent, so search engines generally don't transfer full ranking authority to the new URL. It should only be used when a page will return to its original location later.
404 Error
A status code returned when a requested page doesn't exist on the server. Too many uncontrolled 404s can waste crawl budget and create a poor user experience if not properly redirected or cleaned up.
Above the Fold
The portion of a webpage visible to a user without scrolling, on whatever device they're using. Content and calls to action placed here tend to get the most visibility and engagement.
Algorithm Update
A change search engines make to how they rank content, ranging from minor tweaks to major "core updates" that can significantly shift rankings. Websites are regularly advised to monitor rankings closely after major updates are announced.
Alt Text
A text description attached to an image, read by screen readers and used by search engines to understand image content. It should describe the image accurately and naturally include relevant keywords where appropriate.
Anchor Text
The clickable, visible text of a hyperlink, which gives both users and search engines context about the linked page's topic. Overly repetitive exact-match anchor text across many links can look manipulative rather than natural.
Anchor Text Ratio
The proportional mix of branded, exact-match, partial-match, and generic anchor text pointing to a page. A natural, varied ratio is considered a healthier signal than heavy concentration in any single anchor type.
Answer Box
A direct, on-page answer to a query displayed at the top of search results without requiring a click. It's one of several zero-click SERP features that have grown alongside AI Overviews and featured snippets.
Answer Engine Optimization (AEO)
The practice of optimizing content specifically to be selected and surfaced by AI-driven answer engines like AI Overviews and conversational assistants. It overlaps with GEO but focuses more narrowly on being the direct, cited answer to a question.

B

Backlink
A hyperlink from one website pointing to another, functioning as a vote of confidence in traditional SEO. Backlink quality and relevance generally matter more than raw quantity when it comes to ranking impact.
Black Hat vs White Hat SEO
Black hat SEO refers to tactics that violate search engine guidelines to manipulate rankings, such as cloaking or link schemes. White hat SEO refers to sustainable, guideline-compliant practices that build genuine value over time.
Bounce Rate
The percentage of visitors who leave a site after viewing only one page without any further interaction. A high bounce rate can signal a content or intent mismatch, though it varies significantly by page type.
Brand Mention
A reference to a company or product name across the web, whether or not it includes a hyperlink. Unlinked brand mentions are increasingly treated as a trust signal, particularly for visibility in AI-generated search results.
Breadcrumb Navigation
A secondary navigation trail showing a user's location within a site's hierarchy, typically displayed near the top of a page. It helps both usability and search engines' understanding of a page's position in the site structure.

C

Canonical Tag
An HTML tag that tells search engines which version of a page should be treated as the authoritative one among duplicates. It's essential for consolidating ranking signals when the same or similar content exists at multiple URLs.
Canonicalization
The overall process of resolving duplicate or near-duplicate URLs into a single, preferred version for indexing purposes. It's handled through canonical tags, redirects, and consistent internal linking.
Chunking (Content Chunking)
The process of breaking content into smaller, self-contained segments that retrieval systems can extract and use independently. Chunk size and structure directly affect how well content performs in AI-powered search and retrieval systems.
Citation (AI Citation)
A reference to a source within an AI-generated answer, functioning as the generative-search equivalent of a search ranking. Being cited accurately and frequently across AI platforms is a core visibility goal in Generative Engine Optimization.
Click Depth
The number of clicks required to reach a page from a site's homepage. Pages sitting too many clicks deep tend to be crawled less frequently and receive less internal link authority.
Click-Through Rate (CTR)
The percentage of people who click a search result after seeing it, calculated as clicks divided by impressions. It's affected by ranking position, title and meta description quality, and the presence of competing SERP features.
Cloaking
A black hat tactic that shows different content to search engines than to actual users, typically to manipulate rankings. It violates search engine guidelines and can result in a manual penalty if detected.
Competitor Gap Analysis
A comparison of a site's keyword rankings and content coverage against competitors to identify missed opportunities. It reveals where competitors rank and a target site doesn't, informing content and link building priorities.
Content Audit
A systematic review of existing content's performance, quality, and relevance, typically used to identify pages worth updating, consolidating, or removing. Regular audits help prevent content decay and duplication across a growing site.
Content Pruning
The deliberate removal or consolidation of low-performing, outdated, or thin content from a site. Pruning can improve overall site quality signals and free up crawl budget for higher-value pages.
Content Refresh
The practice of updating existing content with current information, added depth, or improved structure rather than publishing something new. Refreshing older content is often one of the most cost-effective ways to regain lost traffic.
Content Silo
An organizational structure where content is grouped tightly by topic, with internal links reinforcing that topical boundary. Silos help search engines understand a site's areas of depth and expertise more clearly.
Conversion Rate
The percentage of visitors who complete a desired action, such as a purchase or form submission, out of total visitors. It's the metric that ultimately connects SEO traffic to actual business value.
Conversion Rate Optimization (CRO)
The practice of improving a page's design, copy, or user flow specifically to increase the percentage of visitors who convert. It works alongside SEO by ensuring the traffic earned actually translates into results.
Core Update
A broad, significant change to Google's core ranking algorithm, typically affecting a wide range of websites and query types. Core updates are usually announced publicly and can cause noticeable ranking volatility for several weeks.
Core Web Vitals
A set of three page experience metrics, Largest Contentful Paint, Interaction to Next Paint, and Cumulative Layout Shift, that Google uses as ranking signals. They measure loading speed, responsiveness, and visual stability respectively.
Cornerstone Content
The most important, comprehensive pages on a site that represent its core expertise on a topic. These pages typically receive the most internal links and editorial investment relative to supporting content.
Crawl Budget
The number of pages a search engine crawler will visit on a site within a given timeframe. It becomes a meaningful constraint primarily for very large sites with tens of thousands of pages or more.
Crawl Errors
Issues encountered by a search engine crawler while attempting to access a page, such as server errors or blocked resources. Persistent crawl errors on important pages can prevent them from being indexed or updated.
Crawl Rate
How frequently and how much a search engine crawls a given site, influenced heavily by server response time and reliability. Faster, more stable servers generally sustain a higher crawl rate.
Crawlability
The overall ease with which search engine bots can access and navigate a site's pages. Poor crawlability, from blocked resources to broken navigation, prevents content from ever being evaluated for ranking.
Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS)
A Core Web Vitals metric measuring how much a page's visual layout unexpectedly shifts during loading. Common causes include images without defined dimensions and dynamically injected content pushing elements around.

D

Digital PR
An off-page SEO strategy that pitches newsworthy stories, data, or expert commentary to journalists and publications in exchange for coverage and backlinks. It has become one of the most effective link building tactics available.
Disavow File
A file submitted to a search engine listing backlinks a site owner wants to formally disassociate from, typically used to counter spammy or manipulative links. It's generally reserved for cases with clear penalty risk rather than routine cleanup.
Dofollow Link
The default type of hyperlink that passes ranking authority from the linking page to the destination page. Most backlinks on the web are dofollow unless specifically marked otherwise.
Domain Authority (DA)
A third-party metric, developed by Moz, that estimates a domain's overall ranking strength on a logarithmic scale. It's a directional benchmarking tool rather than a metric search engines themselves use.
Domain Rating (DR)
Ahrefs' equivalent metric to Domain Authority, estimating a domain's backlink-based strength relative to others in its index. Like DA, it's useful for comparison but isn't a direct search engine ranking factor.
Duplicate Content
Identical or near-identical content appearing at multiple URLs, either within a site or across different domains. It can split ranking signals and confuse search engines about which version to index and rank.
Dwell Time
The amount of time a visitor spends on a page after clicking a search result before returning to the results page. Longer dwell time is generally interpreted as a signal of content satisfaction, though it isn't a confirmed direct ranking factor.

E

E-commerce SEO
A specialized branch of SEO focused on optimizing product pages, category structures, and faceted navigation for online stores. It places particular emphasis on managing duplicate content risk from filters and variants.
E-E-A-T
An acronym for Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness, the framework search engines use to assess content and source quality. It's especially weighted for topics affecting health, finance, and safety.
Entity (SEO Entity)
A distinct, identifiable person, place, organization, or concept that search engines and AI systems can recognize and track across the web. Establishing clear entity identity helps a brand be consistently understood across multiple platforms.
Evergreen Content
Content that remains relevant and valuable to readers well beyond its initial publication date, unlike time-sensitive news or trend pieces. It typically requires only periodic refreshing rather than being replaced entirely.
Exit Rate
The percentage of visits to a specific page that were the last page in a session, regardless of how many pages were viewed beforehand. Unlike bounce rate, it accounts for pages that were part of a longer browsing session.

F

Faceted Navigation
Filtering options on category or search pages, typically for e-commerce, that let users narrow results by attributes like size, price, or color. It can generate enormous numbers of near-duplicate URLs if not managed carefully.
Featured Snippet
A highlighted answer box appearing at the top of search results, pulled directly from a ranking page's content. Structuring content to directly and concisely answer a specific question increases the odds of earning one.

G

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)
The practice of optimizing content specifically to be retrieved, synthesized, and cited by AI systems like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews. It emphasizes factual density, clear structure, and credible sourcing over traditional keyword targeting.
Google Business Profile (GBP)
A free business listing that controls how a business appears in Google Search and Maps, including reviews, hours, and location. It's the central input for local pack rankings and local search visibility.
Google Search Console (GSC)
A free Google tool that reports on a site's search performance, indexing status, and technical issues directly from Google's own data. It's the most authoritative source for diagnosing indexing and crawling problems.
GPTBot
OpenAI's web crawler used to gather content for training its language models, distinct from bots used for real-time search retrieval. Site owners can allow or block it independently in robots.txt.
Guest Posting
Contributing an article to another website, typically in exchange for a byline link or author bio link back to your own site. Its value depends heavily on the publishing site's editorial standards and genuine relevance.

H

H1 Tag
The HTML tag marking a page's main heading, typically matching or closely echoing the page's core topic and title tag. Every page should generally have exactly one H1 for clarity.
Header Tags (H1 to H6)
A hierarchy of heading tags used to structure a page's content into logical sections and subsections. Consistent, descriptive header use helps both readers and search engines understand a page's organization.
Hreflang
An HTML attribute that tells search engines which language and regional version of a page to serve to users in different locations. It's essential for multilingual and multi-country sites to avoid content competing against itself.
HTML Sitemap
A page on a website, built for human visitors, listing and linking to the site's main pages in an organized way. It supplements navigation and provides additional internal linking value.
HTTPS
The secure, encrypted version of the HTTP protocol, now treated as a baseline trust and ranking signal. Sites without HTTPS are flagged as insecure by most modern browsers.

I

Image SEO
The practice of optimizing images through descriptive file names, alt text, compression, and proper dimensions for both search visibility and page performance. It contributes to both traditional image search rankings and overall Core Web Vitals scores.
Impressions
The number of times a page appeared in search results for a given query, regardless of whether it was clicked. It's tracked alongside clicks and CTR in tools like Google Search Console.
Index Coverage
A report showing which of a site's pages are indexed, excluded, or experiencing errors, along with the reasons for each status. It's one of the most useful diagnostic tools for identifying indexing problems at scale.
Indexation
The process by which a search engine stores a crawled page in its index, making it eligible to appear in search results. Being crawled doesn't guarantee indexation, since quality and duplication concerns can still exclude a page.
Indexed Page
A page that has been successfully added to a search engine's index and is therefore eligible to rank. The ratio of indexed pages to total submitted pages is a useful health metric for larger sites.
Internal Link
A hyperlink connecting one page on a site to another page on the same site. Internal links distribute authority, establish topical relationships, and guide both users and crawlers to important content.
Interstitial
A pop-up or overlay that appears before or during a user's interaction with a page, often used for ads or email sign-ups. Intrusive interstitials, particularly on mobile, can negatively affect both user experience and rankings.

K

Keyword Cannibalization
A situation where multiple pages on the same site unintentionally compete for the same keyword, diluting ranking potential for all of them. It's typically resolved by consolidating overlapping pages or clarifying each page's distinct target intent.
Keyword Density
The frequency of a target keyword's appearance relative to a page's total word count. Modern SEO treats density as far less important than natural language coverage and topical relevance.
Keyword Difficulty (KD)
A score, usually on a 0 to 100 scale, estimating how hard it would be to rank for a given keyword based on competing pages' authority. It's a useful prioritization filter, particularly for newer or lower-authority sites.
Keyword Gap Analysis
A comparison identifying keywords competitors rank for that a given site doesn't, surfacing content opportunities. It's a core tactical method within broader competitor SEO strategy.
Keyword Mapping
The process of assigning specific target keywords to specific pages, ensuring each page has a clear, non-overlapping purpose. It prevents both content gaps and unintentional keyword cannibalization across a site.
Keyword Research
The process of discovering and evaluating search terms relevant to a business, based on volume, difficulty, and intent. It forms the foundation for content planning and prioritization decisions.
Keyword Stuffing
The outdated, penalized practice of overloading a page with a target keyword in an unnatural, repetitive way. Modern algorithms recognize and devalue this tactic rather than rewarding it.
Knowledge Graph
A structured database of entities and their relationships that search engines use to understand facts about people, places, and things. It powers knowledge panels and helps disambiguate similarly named entities.
Knowledge Panel
An information box appearing in search results, typically pulled from a search engine's knowledge graph, summarizing key facts about an entity. It commonly appears for well-established brands, public figures, and organizations.

L

Largest Contentful Paint (LCP)
A Core Web Vitals metric measuring how long it takes for the largest visible content element on a page to fully render. Google's current threshold for a good score is under 2.5 seconds.
Lazy Loading
A technique that delays loading images or other resources until they're about to enter the user's viewport. It improves initial page load speed, particularly on content-heavy pages.
Link Building
The deliberate practice of acquiring backlinks from other websites to build authority and improve rankings. Sustainable link building relies on earning links through genuine value rather than manipulative tactics.
Link Equity (Link Juice)
The ranking value passed from one page to another through a hyperlink. It's distributed based on factors including the linking page's authority, relevance, and the number of other links on that page.
Link Farm
A network of websites created solely to link to each other and artificially inflate backlink counts. It's a manipulative tactic that violates search engine guidelines and carries real penalty risk.
Link Velocity
The rate at which a site acquires new backlinks over time. Unnaturally sudden spikes in link velocity can appear suspicious, while steady, organic growth is generally viewed favorably.
LLM (Large Language Model)
The type of AI model, such as GPT or Claude, that powers modern conversational search and generative answer engines. Understanding how LLMs retrieve and synthesize content is central to Generative Engine Optimization.
Local Citation
A mention of a business's name, address, and phone number on another website, such as a directory or review platform. Consistent citations across the web reinforce trust for local search rankings.
Local Pack
The map-based block of local business results, typically showing three listings, that appears for location-relevant searches. It's driven primarily by Google Business Profile signals rather than traditional organic ranking factors.
Local SEO
The practice of optimizing a business's online presence to rank for location-based searches. It combines Google Business Profile management, local citations, reviews, and location-specific content.
Log File Analysis
The review of a server's raw access logs to see exactly which pages search engine crawlers actually requested and how they responded. It provides more accurate crawl visibility than standard analytics tools.
Long-Form Content
Content that goes into significant depth on a topic, typically well beyond a couple thousand words. It tends to correlate with more backlinks and stronger topical authority when executed well.
Long-Tail Keyword
A longer, more specific search phrase, typically three or more words, with lower individual search volume but higher conversion rates. Long-tail keywords collectively make up the large majority of all search queries.

M

Manual Action (Penalty)
A direct intervention by a search engine's team, rather than an algorithm, to demote or remove a site from search results for violating guidelines. It requires a specific remediation and reconsideration request to resolve.
Meta Description
The short summary text displayed beneath a page's title in search results, written to encourage clicks. It doesn't directly affect rankings but meaningfully influences click-through rate.
Meta Robots Tag
An HTML tag that instructs search engines on how to handle a specific page, such as whether to index it or follow its links. It's commonly used to exclude low-value pages like internal search results from indexing.
Mobile-First Indexing
Google's practice of using the mobile version of a site's content for indexing and ranking, rather than the desktop version. It makes mobile page experience and content parity a critical ranking consideration.

N

NAP Consistency
The consistency of a business's Name, Address, and Phone number across all directories, citations, and platforms where it appears. Inconsistent NAP data can undermine local search trust and rankings.
Nofollow Link
A hyperlink marked with an attribute telling search engines not to pass ranking authority to the destination page. It's commonly used for paid links, comments, and other content a site doesn't want to formally endorse.
Noindex Tag
A directive telling search engines not to include a specific page in their index, even if it's crawled. It's used for pages that should remain accessible but shouldn't appear in search results.

O

Off-Page SEO
All the optimization actions taken outside a website itself to improve its authority and trust, primarily backlinks, brand mentions, and reviews. It complements on-page SEO by building the third-party validation search engines rely on.
On-Page SEO
All the optimization work done directly on a website's pages, including content, titles, headers, and internal links. It's the layer entirely within a site owner's direct control.
Organic Traffic
Visitors who arrive at a site through unpaid search engine results, as opposed to paid advertising or referral traffic. It remains the primary metric most SEO programs are ultimately measured against.
Orphan Page
A page with no internal links pointing to it from anywhere else on the site. Orphaned pages are harder for crawlers to discover and receive no internal link authority, even if technically indexed.

P

Page Authority (PA)
Moz's page-level equivalent of Domain Authority, estimating an individual page's likely ranking strength based on its backlink profile. Like DA, it's a comparative benchmarking metric rather than a search engine ranking factor.
Page Experience
A broad set of signals, including Core Web Vitals, mobile-friendliness, and safe browsing status, that Google uses to evaluate a page's usability. It functions as a ranking tiebreaker between pages of similar content quality.
PageRank
Google's original algorithm for measuring a page's importance based on the quantity and quality of links pointing to it. While the specific public PageRank score is retired, the underlying link-based authority concept still informs modern ranking systems.
Pagination
The division of a long list or set of content across multiple sequential pages, such as page 2 or page 3 of search results or product listings. Proper pagination handling prevents duplicate content and crawl inefficiency issues.
Passage Ranking
A search engine capability that allows specific passages within a page to rank for a query, rather than evaluating the page only as a whole unit. It rewards content with clearly extractable, self-contained answers within longer pages.
People Also Ask (PAA)
A SERP feature displaying a expandable list of related questions and their answers, often sourced from other ranking pages. Appearing here offers additional visibility beyond the standard ranked results.
Pillar Page
A comprehensive page covering a broad topic at a high level, linking out to more specific supporting cluster content. It anchors a topic cluster's internal linking structure and reinforces topical authority.
Private Blog Network (PBN)
A group of websites secretly owned and controlled by one entity, built specifically to link to a "money site" and manipulate its rankings. It's a high-risk, guideline-violating tactic that can trigger significant penalties if discovered.
Prompt (AI Prompt)
The natural-language question or instruction a user enters into an AI system like ChatGPT or Google's AI Mode. Understanding real prompt phrasing, rather than short keyword phrases, is increasingly central to search strategy.

Q

Query
The actual search term or phrase a user types into a search engine. Queries are grouped and analyzed to inform keyword strategy, distinct from the broader "keyword" concept used for targeting.

R

Ranking Factor
Any signal a search engine's algorithm considers when determining a page's position in search results, from backlinks to page speed to content relevance. Google has confirmed it evaluates hundreds of ranking factors in combination.
Rank Tracking
The ongoing monitoring of a site's search position for target keywords over time. Modern rank tracking tools increasingly also monitor visibility within AI Overviews and conversational AI platforms.
Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)
A method some AI search systems use to combine relevance scores across multiple related sub-queries into a single, aggregated ranking. Content that performs well across several related query phrasings benefits disproportionately under this method.
Redirect Chain
A sequence of multiple redirects between the original URL and its final destination, rather than a single direct hop. Each additional redirect adds latency and can dilute link equity passed through the chain.
Redirect Map
A documented plan mapping every old URL to its new destination during a site migration or restructuring. A thorough redirect map is essential for preserving rankings and link equity through structural changes.
Referring Domain
A unique website linking to a given site, regardless of how many individual backlinks come from that domain. Referring domain count is generally considered a more meaningful authority signal than raw total backlink count.
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
A technique where an AI system retrieves relevant external content before generating a response, rather than relying solely on its trained knowledge. Understanding how RAG systems chunk and retrieve content is foundational to GEO strategy.
Rich Snippet
A search result enhanced with additional visual elements, such as star ratings or pricing, generated from structured data markup. It typically improves click-through rate compared to a standard text-only listing.
Robots.txt
A text file at a site's root directory that instructs crawlers which parts of the site they may or may not access. It's the primary tool for controlling both traditional search bots and AI crawlers.

S

Schema Markup
Structured data code added to a page's HTML that explicitly describes its content to search engines in a standardized format. It supports rich results and helps both traditional and AI search systems parse content unambiguously.
Search Intent
The underlying purpose behind a search query, typically categorized as informational, navigational, commercial, or transactional. Matching content to the correct intent is now a prerequisite for ranking competitively.
Search Volume
The estimated number of times a given keyword is searched within a specific timeframe, usually reported monthly. Tool-based volume estimates should be treated as directional rather than exact.
Semantic Search
A search approach that interprets the meaning and context behind a query, rather than matching only exact keyword phrases. It allows search engines to return relevant results even when a page doesn't use the searcher's exact wording.
SERP (Search Engine Results Page)
The page displayed after a user submits a search query, containing organic results, ads, and various SERP features. Its composition varies significantly by query type and intent.
SERP Features
Non-standard result formats appearing on a search results page, including featured snippets, knowledge panels, local packs, and AI Overviews. Their growing presence has reduced the click share available to traditional organic listings.
SERP Volatility
The degree to which search rankings fluctuate day to day, often used as an early indicator of an algorithm update in progress. Elevated volatility across many sites simultaneously typically signals a broader ranking system change.
Session Duration
The total length of time a user spends actively engaged with a site during a single visit. Longer sessions generally correlate with stronger content engagement, though the relationship to rankings isn't direct or confirmed.
Share of Search
A brand's proportion of category search volume relative to its direct competitors, used as a leading indicator of market share trends. It's calculated by dividing a brand's search volume by the total search volume across all competing brands.
Share of Voice
A brand's proportion of overall visibility within a marketing channel, such as organic search or advertising, compared to competitors. Unlike share of search, it can include paid exposure rather than only organic search demand.
Site Architecture
The overall structural organization of a website's pages and how they connect to one another through navigation and internal links. A logical, shallow architecture improves both crawl efficiency and user navigation.
Site Migration
A significant change to a site's domain, URL structure, platform, or design, requiring careful planning to preserve existing rankings. Migrations carry meaningful risk if redirects and technical signals aren't managed precisely.
Site Speed
How quickly a page's content loads and becomes usable for visitors. It affects both user experience and search rankings through its close relationship with Core Web Vitals metrics.
Sitemap (XML Sitemap)
A file listing a site's indexable URLs, submitted to search engines to aid content discovery. It's especially valuable for large sites or those with limited external links pointing into them.
Soft 404
A page that returns a normal 200 status code but has no meaningful content, functioning effectively like an error page without formally signaling as one. Soft 404s can confuse indexing and waste crawl budget if left unaddressed.
Spam Score
A metric estimating the likelihood that a domain exhibits characteristics associated with spammy or manipulative SEO practices. It's typically used as a filter when evaluating potential link building or partnership opportunities.
Structured Data
Standardized markup, based on vocabularies like Schema.org, that explicitly labels the meaning of content elements on a page. It underpins rich results and increasingly supports how AI systems parse and trust content.
Subdomain vs Subdirectory
Two different ways to organize distinct sections of a website, either as a separate subdomain or a folder path within the main domain. Subdirectories generally consolidate authority more efficiently, while subdomains offer more structural separation.

T

Technical SEO
The optimization of a site's underlying infrastructure, crawlability, indexation, speed, and structured data, so search engines can access and understand it reliably. It functions as the foundational layer every other SEO effort depends on.
Thin Content
Pages offering little unique value, depth, or differentiation, often flagged by search engines as low quality. Thin content frequently fails to earn indexing even when technically accessible and crawlable.
Title Tag
The HTML element defining a page's title, displayed as the clickable headline in search results. It remains one of the strongest on-page ranking signals and a major driver of click-through rate.
Topical Authority
The degree to which a site is recognized as a comprehensive, trustworthy source on a specific subject area, built through depth and breadth of related content. It's reinforced through topic clusters and consistent internal linking.
Toxic Backlink
A link from a spammy, low-quality, or penalized site that could negatively affect the linked-to site's trust signals. Toxic backlinks are typically addressed through a disavow file when there's clear penalty risk.
TTFB (Time to First Byte)
The time it takes for a browser to receive the first byte of data from a server after a request. A slow TTFB delays every subsequent stage of page loading, directly affecting Core Web Vitals scores.

U

URL Slug
The final, readable portion of a URL identifying a specific page, typically following the domain and any folder structure. A clean, descriptive slug improves both user clarity and search engine understanding of a page's topic.
User-Generated Content (UGC)
Content created by users rather than a site's own team, such as reviews, forum posts, and comments. It can contribute authenticity and freshness signals but requires moderation to prevent spam or low-quality contributions.

V

Vector Embedding
A numerical representation of text's meaning, used by AI and semantic search systems to compare and retrieve content based on conceptual similarity rather than exact keyword matches. It's the underlying mechanism behind most modern retrieval and RAG systems.
Voice Search Optimization
The practice of optimizing content for spoken, conversational queries typically made through voice assistants. It emphasizes natural language phrasing and direct, concise answers over traditional keyword-focused writing.

W

Webmaster Guidelines
The official rules search engines publish outlining acceptable and prohibited SEO practices. Violating these guidelines risks manual penalties or algorithmic devaluation of affected pages.

Z

Zero-Click Search
A search query that gets resolved directly on the results page, through a featured snippet, AI Overview, or similar feature, without the user clicking through to any website. Zero-click behavior varies significantly by query intent, with informational searches affected far more than transactional ones.
