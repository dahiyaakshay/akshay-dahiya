GEO Glossary

Essential terms for Generative Engine Optimisation, covering AI crawlers, retrieval, citation, entities, and how AI systems find and cite content.

ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWZ

A

AI Assistant Recommendation
When a conversational AI suggests a specific product, service, or business in response to a query. Recommendation likelihood depends on entity clarity, review signals, and citation frequency across the web.
AI Crawler
A bot operated by an AI company to fetch and index web content for training or retrieval. Examples include GPTBot, PerplexityBot, and ClaudeBot.
AI Overviews
Google's AI-generated summary shown above traditional results for many queries. It synthesises information from multiple sources and cites a subset of them directly.
AI Retrievability Audit
A structured assessment of how easily a site's content can be crawled, parsed, and cited by AI systems. It typically evaluates technical access, content structure, and entity clarity.
AI Search Engine
A search or answer platform that uses large language models to generate synthesised responses rather than a ranked list of links. Perplexity, ChatGPT Search, and Google AI Overviews are common examples.
AI Visibility Score
A composite metric estimating how often and how prominently a brand appears in AI-generated answers. It is usually calculated across multiple prompts, industries, and engines.
Answer Box
A direct, extracted answer shown at the top of a results page or chat response. It is typically pulled from a single high-relevance passage on a source page.
Answer Engine Optimization (AEO)
The practice of structuring content so it can be directly extracted and presented as a definitive answer. It overlaps heavily with GEO but focuses specifically on direct-answer formats.
API-based Retrieval
When an AI system fetches content through a structured API call rather than a traditional web crawl. This often bypasses standard on-page SEO signals entirely.
Author Bio
A short biographical statement identifying the writer of a piece, often including credentials. It contributes to E-E-A-T signals used to assess source credibility.

B

Backlink
A hyperlink from one site pointing to another, traditionally used as a ranking signal in classic SEO. In GEO, backlinks matter less than being directly cited as a source within AI-generated text.
BM25
A ranking function estimating document relevance based on term frequency and document length. It remains a foundational component in many retrieval and hybrid search systems.
Bot Traffic
Non-human visits to a website, which can include AI crawlers gathering content for training or live retrieval. Distinguishing legitimate AI bot traffic from other automated traffic matters for access control decisions.
Brand Mentions
Any reference to a brand across the web, whether linked or unlinked. AI systems can draw on brand mentions as evidence of relevance even without a direct hyperlink.
Brand Sentiment
The overall positive, negative, or neutral tone associated with a brand across ingested content. It can influence whether and how a brand is recommended in conversational responses.
Byline
The line of text crediting an article's author, typically near the headline. It supports authorship transparency, a factor in trust-based ranking systems.

C

Cached Retrieval
When an AI system pulls information from a previously stored index rather than fetching a live version of a page. This can cause a lag between content updates and their reflection in AI answers.
Canonical Tag
An HTML element telling search engines which version of a page is authoritative. It helps prevent duplicate content from splitting citation credit across near-identical URLs.
Chunking
The process of breaking a document into smaller segments for indexing and retrieval, rather than treating the whole page as one unit. Chunk boundaries directly affect what gets surfaced in an AI answer.
Citation Probability
The likelihood that a source will be cited by an AI system in response to a defined query set. It is typically estimated empirically by running repeated prompts and measuring citation frequency.
Citation Rate
The proportion of relevant queries in which a domain or page appears as a cited source. It is one of the core measurable outcomes in GEO research.
Citation Source
The specific webpage or document an AI system references when generating part of its answer. A single response may draw on citation sources from many different domains.
Citation-worthy Content
Content structured and substantiated well enough that an AI system is likely to reference it directly. It typically combines clear claims, credible data, and extractable formatting.
ClaudeBot
Anthropic's web crawler, used to gather publicly available content for model training. Site owners can allow or block it via robots.txt.
Co-occurrence
The frequency with which two entities or terms appear together within the same content. High co-occurrence between a brand and a topic strengthens the model's association between them.
Competitive Benchmarking (AI)
Comparing a brand's AI visibility and citation rate against direct competitors across the same prompts. It highlights where a competitor is being cited and a brand is not.
Content Chunk
An individual segment of a larger document created during chunking. Each chunk is indexed and retrieved independently, so its self-contained clarity matters.
Content Comprehensiveness
The degree to which content fully covers a topic rather than addressing it partially. Comprehensive content tends to satisfy more query variations from a single page.
Content Depth
A measure of how thoroughly a topic is explored, including supporting detail and nuance. Greater depth increases the surface area for potential AI citation.
Content Freshness
How recently content was published or meaningfully updated. AI systems often weight freshness heavily for time-sensitive topics.
Content Freshness Signal
The specific metadata cue, such as a publish date, indicating how current a page is. These signals help crawlers prioritise which pages to re-index.
Content Gaps
Topics a brand's content does not currently address, leaving room for competitors to be cited instead. Identifying gaps is a common first step in GEO strategy work.
Content Similarity Score
A numerical measure of how closely two pieces of content resemble each other, often via cosine similarity. It helps detect near-duplicate content across a site.
Conversational AI
A system designed to interact through natural, back-and-forth dialogue rather than single-turn queries. Its multi-turn nature changes how context and prior turns affect retrieval.
Conversational Query
A search input phrased as natural spoken language rather than a short keyword string. These queries are increasingly common on AI platforms and often require more context to answer.
Core Web Vitals
A set of Google metrics measuring real-world page experience, including loading speed and stability. Poor scores can affect crawl efficiency and, indirectly, retrieval likelihood.
Cosine Similarity
A mathematical measure of the angle between two vectors, used to determine semantic similarity. It underpins most modern semantic search and embedding comparison.
Crawl Budget
The finite crawling activity a search engine or AI bot allocates to a site within a time period. Sites with excessive low-value pages can burn through budget before important pages are crawled.
Crawl Freshness
How recently a site's content was last crawled and re-indexed by a given bot. Stale crawl freshness means an AI system may be citing outdated information.
Crawlability
The technical ease with which a bot can access and parse a site's content. Blocked resources, broken links, or heavy JavaScript rendering can all reduce crawlability.
Cross-engine Visibility
The extent to which a brand appears consistently across multiple AI platforms rather than just one. Strong cross-engine visibility reduces dependency on any single system's algorithm changes.
Cross-referencing
When an AI system checks a claim against multiple independent sources before including it in a response. Content corroborated elsewhere is more likely to be trusted and cited.

D

Data-backed Content
Content that supports its claims with original or cited statistics rather than assertions alone. It tends to be favoured for citation because it offers concrete, checkable substance.
Direct Answer
A concise, self-contained response requiring no further reading. AI systems favour content structured to deliver a direct answer within the first few sentences.
Domain Authority
A third-party score estimating a site's overall trustworthiness and ranking strength. It correlates loosely, but not perfectly, with AI citation likelihood.
Duplicate Content
Substantially identical content appearing across multiple URLs, either within a site or across the web. It can dilute citation signals by splitting relevance across several pages.

E

E-E-A-T
Google's framework for evaluating Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness. AI systems appear to weigh similar signals when selecting which sources to cite.
Embedding Model
A neural network that converts text into numerical vectors representing its meaning. Its quality directly affects how accurately semantic search can match queries to content.
Embedding Space
The multi-dimensional mathematical space in which text embeddings are plotted. Semantically related content sits closer together within this space.
Entity
A distinct, identifiable person, place, organisation, product, or concept referenced in content. AI systems parse entities to build structured understanding beyond simple keyword matching.
Entity Density
The concentration of distinct, relevant entities within a piece of content. Higher density can improve topical clarity but must be balanced against readability.
Entity Recognition
The process by which a system identifies and classifies named entities within text. It is a foundational step in how AI models extract structured meaning from unstructured content.
Entity Salience
A measure of how central an entity is to the overall meaning of a document. High salience means an entity is a primary subject, not just a passing mention.
Extractability
How easily a fact, statistic, or claim can be pulled from a page and used in an AI answer. Clear formatting and self-contained statements improve extractability.

F

Fact Verification
The process an AI system may use to confirm a claim is accurate before including it in a response. Content independently corroborated is more likely to pass this check.
Featured Snippet
A highlighted result showing a direct excerpt from a page, typically above standard organic results. It served as an early precursor to today's AI-generated answer boxes.
First-party Data
Original data collected directly by a brand or publisher, rather than sourced from third parties. It is highly valued in GEO because it cannot be found duplicated elsewhere on the web.

G

Gap Analysis (AI Visibility)
A structured comparison identifying which prompts or topics a brand fails to appear in versus competitors. It forms the basis for prioritising future content work.
Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)
The practice of optimising content so it is more likely to be retrieved, cited, and surfaced by generative AI systems. It extends traditional SEO principles into the context of AI-generated answers.
GPTBot
OpenAI's web crawler used to collect content for model training. Website owners can permit or restrict its access through robots.txt.

H

Hallucination (AI)
When a language model generates information that is factually incorrect or entirely fabricated. Strong grounding and source citation are key defences against hallucinated outputs.
HTML-to-text Conversion
The process of stripping markup from a webpage to extract its readable textual content. How cleanly this conversion happens affects what an AI system actually "sees" of a page.

I

Informational Query
A search intent aimed at learning something rather than completing a transaction. These queries are the most common type surfaced through AI-generated answers.
Internal Linking
The practice of linking between pages within the same site to establish topical relationships. It helps both crawlers and AI systems understand site structure and hierarchy.

J

JavaScript Rendering Issues
Problems occurring when a crawler cannot fully execute a page's JavaScript, resulting in incomplete content being indexed. Many AI crawlers have limited rendering capability compared to modern browsers.
JSON-LD
A lightweight structured data format embedded in a webpage's code to describe content in machine-readable terms. It is the most commonly recommended method for implementing schema markup.

K

Keyword Matching
A retrieval method identifying relevant content based on exact or near-exact term overlap with a query. It is simpler than semantic search but remains a component in many hybrid retrieval systems.
Knowledge Graph
A structured database of entities and the relationships between them, used to power more contextual results. Google's Knowledge Graph underlies many of its direct-answer features.
Knowledge Panel
An information box summarising key facts about an entity, typically sourced from a knowledge graph. It represents a well-established, high-confidence data source.

L

Large Language Model (LLM)
A neural network trained on vast amounts of text to generate and understand natural language. LLMs form the underlying engine behind most modern AI search and chat platforms.
Lexical Matching
A method of comparing text based on exact word overlap rather than underlying meaning. It is faster than semantic matching but can miss conceptually relevant content phrased differently.
Link Equity
The value passed between pages through a hyperlink, traditionally used to influence rankings. Its role in AI citation is less direct but still contributes to overall domain trust.
Local Business Recommendation (AI)
When a conversational AI suggests a specific local business for a location-based query. It typically depends on consistent business information and positive review signals.
Local SEO for AI
The practice of optimising a business's online presence so it is more likely to be recommended by AI systems. NAP consistency and review volume are central factors.
Long-tail Query
A specific, often multi-word query with lower search volume but higher intent clarity. AI systems handle long-tail queries particularly well due to their conversational design.

M

Markdown Extraction
The process of converting a webpage's HTML into simplified Markdown for easier machine parsing. Many AI research workflows use this format to standardise content before analysis.
Mobile-first Indexing
Google's practice of using the mobile version of a page as the primary basis for indexing. Sites with poor mobile experiences risk incomplete content capture.
Multi-hop Retrieval
A retrieval process requiring information from more than one source to fully answer a query. It is common in complex questions no single page fully addresses.
Multi-modal Retrieval
Retrieval spanning multiple content types, such as text, images, and video, rather than text alone. It is becoming more relevant as AI systems increasingly ingest non-textual content.
Multi-source Synthesis
When an AI system combines information from several sources into a single coherent answer. This is a defining behaviour separating generative search from traditional link lists.

N

NAP Consistency (Name, Address, Phone)
The requirement that a business's core details match exactly across every online listing. Inconsistencies can weaken an AI system's confidence when recommending a local business.
Named Entity Recognition (NER)
An NLP technique for identifying and classifying entities such as people, places, and organisations. It underlies much of how AI systems structure unstructured content.
Noindex Tag
An HTML directive instructing search engines not to include a page in their index. Pages marked noindex are typically excluded from AI citation since most systems respect the directive.

O

Original Research
Content based on data not previously published elsewhere, such as surveys or studies. It is among the strongest signals for earning AI citation because it cannot be sourced from a competitor.

P

Page Load Signals
Metrics related to how quickly and reliably a page loads for a visitor or crawler. Poor load signals can reduce crawl frequency and depth over time.
Passage Ranking
The process of scoring individual passages within a document, rather than the whole page. It allows a single long page to surface only its most relevant section.
Passage Retrieval
The retrieval of a specific segment of text to answer a query, rather than returning the whole source. It is central to how modern AI answers are assembled.
PerplexityBot
Perplexity AI's web crawler, used to gather content for its retrieval-augmented answer engine. It can be allowed or blocked through standard robots.txt configuration.
Perplexity AI
A conversational AI search engine that generates answers with inline citations drawn from live web retrieval. It is widely studied in GEO research due to its transparent citation behaviour.
Prompt
The input text a user submits to an AI system to generate a response. Prompt wording significantly influences which sources are retrieved and cited.
Prompt Engineering
The practice of carefully crafting input prompts to produce more accurate or useful AI outputs. In GEO research, it is used to systematically test citation behaviour across query variations.
Prompt Injection
An attempt to manipulate an AI system's behaviour by embedding hidden instructions within content it processes. It is a security concern distinct from, but occasionally relevant to, content optimisation.
Prompt Universe
The full set of representative prompts used to test a brand's visibility across an industry or topic area. Building a comprehensive prompt universe is a foundational step in AI visibility research.
Publish Date Metadata
The structured or visible date indicating when content was first published. It helps AI systems assess freshness relative to competing sources.

Q

Query Expansion
The process of adding related terms or concepts to a search query to broaden retrieval coverage. AI systems often perform this internally before executing a search.
Query Fan-Out
When a single prompt is internally broken into multiple sub-queries to gather broader supporting information. It allows a more complete synthesised answer than one search alone would provide.
Query Intent
The underlying goal a user has when submitting a query, such as learning, buying, or navigating. Correctly matching content to intent is essential for both traditional and AI-driven retrieval.
Query Reformulation
When a system or user rewords a query to improve retrieval results, often after an unsatisfactory response. AI systems may reformulate queries automatically as part of multi-step reasoning.

R

Real-time Retrieval
When an AI system fetches live, current web information at the moment of a query, rather than relying only on training data. It is a key differentiator of retrieval-augmented systems.
Retrievability
The general ease with which content can be found and surfaced by AI retrieval systems. It combines technical crawlability with content clarity and relevance.
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
An architecture where a language model retrieves relevant external documents before generating a response, rather than relying only on trained knowledge. Most modern AI search engines are built on some form of RAG.
Review Signals
Aggregate data from customer reviews, including volume, recency, and sentiment. These signals strongly influence whether an AI system recommends a local business or product.
Robots Meta Tag
An HTML tag controlling how search engines may index or follow links on a specific page. It offers page-level control distinct from site-wide robots.txt rules.
Robots.txt
A text file at a site's root instructing bots which parts of the site they may or may not crawl. It is the primary mechanism for allowing or blocking specific AI crawlers.

S

Semantic Chunk Boundaries
The points at which a document is split during chunking, ideally aligned with natural topic shifts. Poorly chosen boundaries can separate a claim from its supporting context.
Semantic Duplication
When two pieces of content convey the same meaning using different wording, rather than being textually identical. It can still dilute citation signals despite not being flagged as literal duplicate content.
Semantic Relevance
How closely the meaning of content aligns with the intent behind a query, independent of exact wording. It is the foundation of modern retrieval systems built on embeddings.
Semantic Search
A search approach matching queries to content based on meaning rather than exact keyword overlap. It relies on vector embeddings and similarity scoring rather than lexical matching alone.
Sentiment Analysis
The automated process of determining whether text expresses positive, negative, or neutral sentiment. It gauges brand perception embedded within the content AI models draw upon.
SERP (Search Engine Results Page)
The page displayed after a search query, traditionally a ranked list of links. AI Overviews and answer boxes increasingly occupy the top of the modern SERP.
Server-side Rendering (SSR)
A web architecture where pages are fully rendered on the server before being sent to the browser or crawler. It generally improves crawlability compared to client-side-only rendering.
Share of Voice (AI)
The proportion of AI-generated answers in which a brand appears relative to competitors across a defined prompt set. It is one of the primary comparative metrics in GEO measurement.
Sitemap.xml
An XML file listing a site's important pages to help crawlers discover and prioritise content. It supports, but does not guarantee, more efficient crawling.
Source Attribution
The practice of an AI system explicitly naming or linking the origin of information within its response. Clear attribution builds user trust and drives referral traffic from AI answers.
Source Credibility
The perceived trustworthiness of a piece of content's origin, based on authorship, domain reputation, and citation by others. Higher credibility increases the likelihood of being selected as a citation source.
Static Site Generation (SSG)
A web architecture where pages are pre-built as static HTML rather than generated dynamically per request. It typically offers strong crawlability and fast load times for AI bots.
Statistical Content
Content including quantitative data, such as percentages or measured outcomes. It tends to be favoured for citation because numbers are easily extractable and verifiable.
Structured Data
Standardised markup added to a webpage to explicitly describe its content and meaning to machines. It helps AI systems parse entities, relationships, and facts more reliably than prose alone.

T

TF-IDF
A statistical measure weighing a term's frequency in a document against how common it is across all documents. It remains a widely used method for lexical relevance scoring.
Third-party Mentions
References to a brand made by external websites rather than the brand's own content. They serve as independent corroboration that strengthens an AI system's confidence in a claim.
Topic Cluster
A group of related content pieces organised around a central pillar topic, typically interlinked. It helps establish comprehensive topical coverage AI systems can recognise as authoritative.
Topical Authority
The degree to which a site is recognised as a comprehensive, trustworthy source on a subject. It is built through consistent, in-depth coverage over time rather than a single piece of content.
Training Data Cutoff
The date after which an AI model has no direct knowledge from its original training. Content published after this date can only be surfaced through live retrieval rather than internal knowledge.
Transactional Query
A search intent focused on completing an action, such as a purchase. AI systems handling these queries often prioritise up-to-date pricing and availability information.
Trust Signals
Any indicator contributing to confidence in a piece of content, such as author credentials or citation by reputable sources. They are central to both traditional SEO and GEO evaluation.

U

Unlinked Mentions
References to a brand that appear in text without an accompanying hyperlink. AI systems can still register these as relevant signals even without a direct link.
Update Frequency
How often content is revised or refreshed after initial publication. Regularly updated content is generally favoured for topics where accuracy changes over time.
User-agent Blocking
The practice of restricting specific bots, identified by their user-agent string, from accessing a website. It is commonly used to selectively allow or deny individual AI crawlers.

V

Vector Database
A specialised database designed to store and efficiently search text embeddings based on similarity. It underpins the retrieval step in most modern semantic and AI-powered search systems.
Vector Embeddings
Numerical representations of text capturing semantic meaning in a high-dimensional space. They allow systems to compare content based on meaning rather than exact wording.

W

Wikidata
A free, structured knowledge base that many AI systems and knowledge graphs draw upon for entity data. Accurate representation here can meaningfully influence how an entity is understood by AI systems.
Wikipedia Citation
A reference to a Wikipedia article as a source within an AI-generated answer. Wikipedia remains one of the most heavily cited sources across nearly all major AI search platforms.

Z

Zero-click Search
A search interaction in which the user gets an answer directly within the results page or AI response, without clicking through to a source site. It is one of the central challenges GEO strategies aim to address for driving brand visibility despite reduced referral traffic.
